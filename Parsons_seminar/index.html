Augmented creativity Team

    Our mission is twofold:

    • Theoretical side: Use AI to discover original solutions to complex problems fostering true creativity at the foundations.

    • Applicative side: Apply AI to augment human creativity in real-world contexts empowering creators and innovators.


    In this presentation, we will explore two exciting activities:

    • S+T+ARTS Projects: Our role in a European initiative at the intersection of science, technology, and the arts.

    • Movement Models: Developing AI models inspired by Large Language Models to learn and generate human movement.

What Is Creativity?

Creativity, first, is recombination under rules of coherence — grammar, syntax, structure
  • Type I — generalization within a fixed rule set; the boundary of what is known does not move

  • Type II — changing the rules themselves; same context, new composition rules

  • For us, creativity is the capacity to move between the two — structured enough to stay coherent, unstable enough to genuinely surprise

Can we build systems that actively explore the Adjacent Possible?

  • Human movement is the expression of a complex dynamical system: Multi-timescale, high-dimensional, non-stationary

  • Open-ended enough to be genuinely creative:

    • Postures $\Longrightarrow$ words | Movements $\Longrightarrow$ sentences

    • Movement spans a spectrum of semantic constraint:

      • Expressive domains (dance): high freedom, novelty is not an error

      • Procedural domains (surgery, sport): tight boundaries, deviation has real cost

Duetto - First application and proof of concept of the LMM

    Real-time generative system built on top of a Large Movement Model



    • Can we leverage deep learning to develop a movement suggestion system for dancers?

      • Can Bidirectional dialogue between a human body and a machine. The human moves, the system listens, the system proposes, the human responds.

Why Dance ?

Dance movement is a rich, complex data source

Duetto - First application and proof of concept of the LMM:

  • It captures a person's movement through a pose estimation pipeline, then follows one of two approaches we are exploring:
    • Token discretization via vector quantization
    • Direct pose estimation
    An autoregressive model then generates new movement — token by token — conditioned on both the history of past movement and the live input from the performer.

Setup and procedure
  • Music Stimuli Selection

    • 3 levels of intensity: low, medium, high
    • 3 musical styles: classical, rock, electronic

  • Dancer Selection:

    • Professional dancers
    • Semiprofessional dancers
    • Non-professional dancers

  • Experiment Design:
    • Each music piece is danced at least 3 times to ensure consistency and data reliability.
Preparing the data set for deep learning
  • Movement Data Collection:

    • Movements captured through skeletonization, tracking the position of body joints over time.

  • Audio Feature Extraction:

    • Using Librosa to extract key spectral and rhythm features

Preparing the data set for deep learning


  • Movement captured via sVision — our real-time human pose estimation pipeline

  • Extracts key anatomical landmarks (joints) from video

Approach I: Discrete-Token Autoregressive Model

  • Raw joint coordinates must be transformed into discrete tokens $\Longrightarrow$ posture quantization


  • Our contribution: SOM-VQ — adds spatial structure to the codebook via a Self-Organising Map, mapping each data point to the node whose vector is closest to the input


  • Tokens close together = similar postures $\Longrightarrow$ the codebook becomes a navigable map of movements


  • Enables genuine human-in-the-loop control: steer generation by moving through the map

    • Divergence as Dialogue

  • The spatial gap between the two markers is not an error, it is the creative distance the system is navigating.

    • Approach II: Autoregressive Conditional Variational Autoencoder



    • Data-driven generative models of human movement using autoregressive conditional variational autoencoders

    • The latent variables of the learned autoencoder define the action space for the movement and thereby govern its evolution over time


    The Generative Model

      Critical requirement:

      • Real-time: not cached replay, but live production at every step
      • Human-in-the-loop (HIL) training: the model acts, the dancer reacts, the dancer moves, the model adapts → reciprocal dialogue, not one-way generation
        • $\Longrightarrow$ Autoregressive generation, conditioned on history and live performer input

    Approach I — Discrete-Token Autoregressive Model
    • Pros
      • Simple to train
      • Facilitates human-in-the-loop implementation

    • Cons
      • A smaller "vocabulary" (codebook) results in fewer distinct poses and less fluid movement
    Approach II — Autoregressive CVAE
    • Pros
      • A continuous latent space allows a far larger range of poses, producing more natural movement

    • Cons
      • Human-in-the-loop control is conceptually and computationally more complex to implement

    Kreyon City

    Kreyon City is a macro-economic planning tool that exploits methods from Statistical Physics and Artificial Intelligence to develop forecasting algorithms for urban indicators. With Kreyon City, it is possible to check the evolution of different urban-related dimensions, such as wages, employment, green areas, etc. While the decision-making platform is under construction, Kreyon City has been realised into an installation to study how individuals solve complex urban problems.

    Six degrees of separation

    This work has been presented at the “Ti con Zero” exhibition in 2022 and it explains how recommendation systems, used by online platforms, work to suggest content to us. Through the installation, people can understand the risk of reinforcing our natural tendency not to depart from our personal comfort zone and instead to conform to prevailing fashions, thus ultimately making us more predictable consumers and less curious people.

    Trace your waste

    Trace your waste, the project led by Susi Gutsche, aims to observe and visualise movements of our garbage and explore possible impacts on future urban life within the context of waste. Gaining more knowledge and data in order to understand the usually hidden dynamics can produce a wider discussion and understanding of challenges and problems. How to pave the way to a resilient waste mobility?

    Cities live like trees

    Cities live like trees, the project led by Olga Kisseleva is based on the idea of mapping human emotions from citizens and link them with tree memories and location, as well to provide new spaces in the cities for citizens to recharge. The idea behind is that using the power of trees inter-connectedness, we can repair city spaces, which are full of emotions of solstalgia and nature-lack-disorders. The artistic exploration is based on the combination of citizen science and data processing algorithms.

    SAMASANA, Togetherness

    SAMASANA, Togetherness by Filippo Gregoretti. Amrita, an artificial intelligence protagonist of this work, processes a series of tweets, characterized by a certain kind of toxicity in their argumentation and turns them into an audio-visual experience. The images and sounds it proposes change according to the higher or lower level of toxicity these contents present. What we write on social, has weight and even when we are not aware of it!

    The Body and the City by Michail Ribakov, provides a visualization of “personal space” within cities. Through the observation of bodies moving in urban spaces we understand how often and in what ways this space of our own, enters into relationship with that of others, how often it is invaded or what we are forced to do, in terms of movement to preserve it.

    Sound Dialogues: What Do Cities Tell Us

    Sound Dialogues: What Do Cities Tell Us? by Anthony Tandazo is an experimental audiovisual performance that listens to the city as a living system. Developed in collaboration with NCDIA Unifor during a residency in Fortaleza, the work draws a sonic comparison between two cities under ecological and social pressure: Fortaleza (Brazil) and Guayaquil (Ecuador). Using a custom-coded platform that translates urban dynamics into algorithmic musical compositions, Tandazo engages with technology both as a critical and poetic tool.

    Direito à Sombra (The Right to Shade)

    Direito à Sombra (The Right to Shade) by Meri Nagem explores the relationship between heat, solar incidence, and urban inequality in Fortaleza, where access to shade is closely tied to social vulnerability. What began as a technical inquiry into designing a custom clay cobogó, a traditional perforated Brazilian brick, evolved into a broader recognition of trees as ancestral technologies of shade and care. This shift from fabrication to observation runs through Nagem's project, suggesting technology as a relational and ecological practice, and pointing to the possibility that the most sophisticated response to urban heat may already be rooted in the ground beneath our feet.

    Ulysses's Gates

    In a dialogue between art, science and generative technologies, Ulysses' Gates brings together sound and visual composition, performance, complexity theory and environmental data to transform the phenomena of the lagoon into a sensory experience. In Venice, a city that symbolises the fragile and millennia-old relationship between civilisation and water, Climate-tides finds its ideal starting point. Here, water is protection, threat, memory and dialogue: a presence to be observed, understood and listened to.

    Duetto

    “Duetto” explores body as a form of language. In this perspective, postures become elementary units, while movement unfolds as a complex and expressive sequence, much like meaning emerging through words and sentences. But the project does more than describe motion: it seeks to recognize relations, possibilities and transformations within gesture, opening a space in which AI can not only interpret movement, but also respond to it in creative ways. At the heart of Duetto lies a genuinely human-in-the-loop vision. The human performer does not simply initiate the process but continuously shapes and redirects what the system understands and proposes. Duetto has been presented as an “art&science” performance several times.
